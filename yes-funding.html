Follow the Money

Who is paying to pass the tax?

The Connect Bay Area Transit committee, which is running the Yes on Measure RTM campaign, reported $7,508,233 in itemized contributions from 162 donors on filings through September 28, 2026. Sixty-two donors giving $25,000 or more supplied 96% of that money. About $3 million came from contractors, unions and other organizations that stand to benefit from the spending the tax would fund, and the committee has spent more than $4.1 million on the paid petition drive that put the measure on the ballot.

More than $7.5 million, mostly from those who benefit

The committee's itemized contributions now total more than $7.5 million. The donors fall into a familiar pattern: the contractors who build transit, the firms that design it, the unions that staff it and large employers and individual backers.

The pattern: about $3 million of the $7,508,233 raised came from contractors, consultants and unions with a direct financial stake in transit spending, and most of the rest came from a few dozen large corporations and wealthy individuals. Contributions under $1,000 from ordinary residents, who would pay an extra half percentage point in sales tax for 14 years, total $14,545.

Where the money went: a $4.1 million petition drive

Measure RTM reached the ballot as a citizens' initiative, which needs only a simple majority to pass, rather than as an agency-placed tax requiring two-thirds approval. Qualifying it took a paid signature drive across five counties. Of the $5.13 million in payments of $1,000 or more that the committee reported through September 19, 2026, $4.10 million, or 80%, was coded as petition circulating.

Why it matters: $4.1 million of the $7.5 million the Yes campaign raised, or 55%, went to paid signature gathering, and $3.5 million of that went to a single Las Vegas firm. The corporations, contractors and unions listed above paid for the initiative route, which lets the tax pass with a simple majority instead of the two-thirds vote required for a tax placed on the ballot by a government body.

The coalition behind it

The committee is led by a mix of business and labor organizations, including:

  • Bay Area Council (regional business group)
  • SPUR (urban policy advocacy)
  • SAMCEDA (San Mateo County economic development)
  • SEIU Local 1021 and ATU Local 1555 (transit and public-sector unions)
