The AC Transit "sweetheart deal"

AC Transit's general manager, Michael Hursh, received $556,045 in total pay and benefits in 2022, with more in "other pay" and benefits than any other major California transit chief executive. When he stepped down, the board approved an arrangement that kept him on full general-manager pay through September 2025 as a senior adviser with no defined duties, an arrangement a board member publicly called a "sweetheart deal" and a "gift of public funds." AC Transit also failed to file a compliant 2023 compensation report with the State Controller, so a full accounting of that year is unavailable.

Overtime turns line jobs into six-figure jobs

The highest-paid BART employee in 2022 was not an executive. A power and mechanical section manager earned $433,682, including $246,847 in overtime. In an earlier and widely reported case, a BART system service worker, a janitorial position, turned a base salary of about $57,945 into $271,243 in total pay and benefits in a single year through overtime, after similar payouts in prior years. These are not isolated stories; they reflect work rules and staffing practices the agencies have not reformed.

Why this matters: compensation is the single largest cost in transit budgets. A new 14-year tax would lock in this cost structure rather than reform it. There is a better way →
